What AsterDEX Is

AsterDEX is a decentralized exchange that lets traders swap tokens and open on-chain perpetual positions directly from a self-custody wallet. Rather than routing orders through a centralized order book that takes custody of client funds, it settles every trade through smart contracts, so assets move between a trader's wallet and the protocol's liquidity without a custodial intermediary ever holding them. The platform pairs a spot swap engine with a derivatives layer, giving one interface for straightforward token swaps and for leveraged perpetual contracts. Because every action is a signed on-chain transaction, fills, funding payments and liquidations can all be checked independently on a block explorer rather than taken on trust from an internal company ledger.

AsterDEX's Spot and Perpetual Trading Products

The platform splits its trading surface into two linked products that share the same wallet connection and the same underlying liquidity philosophy: swap now, or open a position and hold it over time.

Spot Swaps on AsterDEX

The spot side works the way most decentralized exchanges do: a trader picks a token pair, the interface quotes a rate drawn from on-chain liquidity, and a single signed transaction exchanges one asset for another at that quoted rate minus slippage. The mechanics follow the same automated-market-maker logic described in the Uniswap documentation, where price moves along a curve as each trade shifts the pool's balance rather than matching two counterparties directly.

Perpetual Futures on AsterDEX

The derivatives side lets a trader open a long or short position with leverage and no expiry date, settling gains, losses and periodic funding payments entirely on-chain. Because positions and collateral sit in transparent contracts, the size and health of open interest can be cross-checked against aggregated DeFi data such as the perpetuals metrics tracked on DefiLlama, rather than relying on a platform's own reporting.

Order Types AsterDEX Supports

A trader here is not limited to a single way of entering or exiting a position; the interface exposes the standard order types a derivatives trader expects alongside the simple swap flow.

Market and Limit Orders

A market order fills immediately at the best available on-chain price, which suits a trader who wants certainty of execution over price precision. A limit order instead sits until the market reaches the chosen price, giving more control at the cost of a fill that may never arrive. Both order types build on the same order-book and matching concepts documented generally for a decentralized exchange, just implemented through this platform's own on-chain contracts.

Leverage and Margin on Perpetual Markets

On the perpetual side, a trader posts collateral and opens a position larger than that collateral through leverage. Higher leverage narrows the distance between entry price and liquidation price, so margin usage is the main lever a trader has over how much room a position carries before it is force-closed. Funding payments periodically transfer between long and short holders to keep the perpetual price tethered to the underlying spot price.

Self-Custody Trading on AsterDEX

Every trade here, whether a spot swap or a perpetual order, is signed from a wallet the trader controls — there is no account to register, no password to reset and no custodial balance sitting on a company's books. A wallet such as one built around Ethereum's wallet standards holds the private keys, and the platform only ever requests a signature for the specific transaction being submitted. This matters most during a liquidation or a market swing: because it never takes possession of the underlying assets, there is no exchange-held balance that can be frozen, mismanaged or lost to an operator's own failure. The trader's only real responsibility is securing that wallet's seed phrase — something that is never asked for and never needed.

Getting Started on AsterDEX

Opening a first trade follows the same short sequence whether the goal is a spot swap or a leveraged position.
  • Connect a self-custody wallet on a supported network.
  • Fund the wallet with the asset needed for the trade or for perpetual collateral.
  • Choose spot swap or perpetual market, then pick a market or limit order.
  • Review the quoted price, fee and, for perpetuals, the liquidation level before signing.
  • Confirm the transaction in the wallet and monitor the open position or completed swap.

AsterDEX Compared to Other Decentralized Exchanges

Many decentralized exchanges specialize in either spot swaps or perpetual futures, rarely both. Its position is to combine them, so a trader who wants to swap into a stablecoin and then open a leveraged position does not need to move between two separate protocols or re-approve tokens on a second interface. That combination does not remove the fundamentals every on-chain venue shares: liquidity depth sets how much a large order moves the price, and leverage on the perpetual side amplifies both gains and losses. Judged against the broader category, it sits alongside other venues built on the same transparent, non-custodial settlement model, where every fill and every funding payment is a public on-chain record rather than an entry in a private ledger.

Yes. Every trade is signed from the trader's own wallet, and the platform never takes possession of the underlying assets at any point.
Can I trade perpetuals and spot assets in the same place on AsterDEX?
Yes. It links a spot swap engine with a perpetual futures layer, so a trader can swap into collateral and open a leveraged position without switching platforms.
What networks does AsterDEX run on?
